Introduction

Spoken speech is the most universal and natural way of human communication. However, in the modern world, diseases leading to temporary or permanent loss of speech ability are increasingly diagnosed[cite: 1]. At the same time, in many cases, patients fully retain consciousness, understanding of addressed speech, and the need for full communication. They continue to use articulation, but the absence of sound during conversation leads to difficulties in communication with medical staff and relatives[cite: 2]. In such cases, there is a need to use augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) tools.

One of the promising areas for the development of such tools are visual speech recognition systems based on neural networks and deep learning methods.

1. Relevance of the Topic

Currently, there are various types of augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) tools, such as sign language, bliss symbols, pictograms, communication boards, the PECS system, and specialized keyboards. However, their use is not always possible due to requirements for the patient's physical activity, as well as the need to use hands and fine motor skills, which is excluded in severe cases of diseases[cite: 3]. Moreover, caregivers often lack the necessary experience and training for effective communication with patients who have lost the ability to speak, and the use of existing AAC tools is limited by a lack of time to establish such understanding, knowledge, and situational specifics. This causes a growing current need for AAC systems for people with voice function impairments, which would allow recognizing words by human lip movements.

2. Purpose, Tasks, and Planned Results of the Study

The purpose of the study is to develop a visual speech recognition system for a limited set of Russian speech commands based on articulation video sequences without using an audio signal.

In connection with this goal, the following tasks are solved in this work:

  1. To study existing solutions in the field of visual speech recognition and AAC systems;
  2. To analyze existing neural network architectures and select a suitable model;
  3. To investigate the possibilities of applying transfer learning methods to adapt the model to the Russian language;
  4. To form a Russian-language dataset for training a neural network;
  5. To adapt the pre-trained model to recognize Russian speech commands using transfer learning;
  6. To evaluate recognition quality;
  7. To develop an AAC web system based on the resulting model.

Object of study: the process of visual recognition of speech units based on video sequences of articulatory movements.

Subject of study: video sequence processing methods and neural network modeling used for visual recognition of a limited set of Russian speech units without using an audio signal.

Within the framework of the master's thesis, it is planned to obtain relevant scientific results in the following areas:

  1. Experimental evaluation of the possibility of transferring knowledge of a model trained on English video data to the task of recognizing a limited set of Russian speech commands;
  2. Evaluation of recognition quality after model adaptation and analysis of characteristic errors when processing Russian-language video sequences;
  3. Identification of features and limitations of applying transfer learning under conditions of a limited volume of Russian-language training data.

The planned practical result of the study is a software system for visual recognition of Russian speech commands with a web interface that allows transmitting a video sequence of articulatory movements, receiving the recognition result, and displaying it to the user. The developed prototype can serve as a basis for the further development of augmentative and alternative communication tools for people with voice function impairments.

3. Review of Research and Developments

3.1 Overview of Global Research

One of the most famous examples of visual speech recognition systems is the SRAVI application, developed by Liopa after years of research. SRAVI is intended for patients with speech impairments after tracheostomy, stroke, paralysis, and other diseases, and is capable of converting silent lip movements into text or audio format. The application can be used on any mobile devices with a front camera. Before starting the recognition process, the device is positioned in front of the patient's face, after which computer vision algorithms automatically detect and track lip movements. As a result, within a few seconds, the three most likely phrase options are displayed on the screen. The SRAVI application is licensed as a medical device, but is not available for individual use; its supply is carried out only upon request from medical organizations. The system's recognition vocabulary is limited to a set of 40 predefined phrases chosen taking into account the patient's basic needs. The accuracy of the application in ideal conditions is 90%, but in a real clinical environment (insufficient lighting, bandages, head tilt), this value decreases. Also, the SRAVI system supports only English, which significantly limits its use for Russian-speaking patients.

Another example of AAC systems for people who have lost the ability to speak due to illness is the Lip2ja application created by a research group at Osaka University. Lip2ja is a lip-reading system integrated with the CoeFont speech platform. During operation, the application uses machine learning algorithms to analyze the position of the lips, subsequently matching the obtained features with words in Japanese. Built-in speech synthesis allows selecting an individual timbre for voicing phrases, as well as recreating the patient's voice from recordings made before surgery. However, due to the phonetic features of the Japanese language, the application recognizes only a limited set of phrases most commonly used in communication between the patient and medical staff.

There is also a specialized VSRICU architecture for lip-reading intensive care patients, developed by researchers at RWTH Aachen University in Germany. Such a model combines two approaches: audio and visual recognition. At the first stage, the neural network uses the input video frames of the patient's face to generate audio features and uses them as intermediate values. At the second stage, based on the obtained characteristics, the final text recognition result is formed. To train the model, the researchers collected a dataset containing 238 phrases in English and German recorded with the participation of intensive care patients. The command vocabulary itself was selected by experienced doctors to take into account the most important topics in communication between the patient, medical staff, and relatives. Such a rejection of publicly available audiovisual datasets made it possible to focus on the specific conditions of a hospital room and simplified the training process of the neural network.

3.2 Overview of National Developments

One of the examples of national research in the field of visual speech recognition is the development by specialists of the St. Petersburg Federal Research Center of the Russian Academy of Sciences (SPC RAS), aimed at improving the accuracy of voice assistants in noisy conditions. At the core of the developed system is a neural network trained to recognize several hundred commands by audiovisual features. Thus, the neural network automatically selects which type of data will provide maximum accuracy. Testing of the system's operation was carried out with the participation of heavy truck drivers. The accuracy of command recognition solely by lip reading was 60-80%, and in combination with an audio signal, more than 90%.

In the work of G.S. Afanasenko, the stages of developing a Russian speech visual recognition system are considered. The LipNet architecture is implemented as a neural network. However, at the time of publication, model training was still ongoing, so quality indicators are not presented in the article.

3.3 Overview of Local Results

Among related studies performed at KubSU, we can note works devoted to face detection and facial landmark detection using neural networks. The work of A.A. Shulga and E.P. Lukashchik considers the task of face detection in images. The publication by D.Yu. Romanyuta and E.P. Lukashchik investigates the task of determining facial landmark points.

4. Development and Optimization of Neural Network Recognition Architecture

Of greatest practical interest is the development of a model adapted to visual speech recognition in Russian. However, there are no publicly available Russian-language datasets that would meet the conditions of the problem under consideration. At the same time, training a neural network from scratch for lip-reading requires a large amount of data, since the language contains many phonemes that are visually indistinguishable from each other. Collecting and annotating a custom Russian-language dataset requires huge expenditures of time and computational resources, which is irrational within the current study. Therefore, the optimal solution is to use the transfer learning method, which involves adapting an English-language model to the specifics of the Russian language.

For this purpose, at the initial stage of the study, a neural network for visual recognition of English words was developed and trained. For its training, the GRID dataset was selected from open and freely available datasets. The GRID dataset is characterized by high data quality, but due to the predefined phrase template, it does not reflect the structure of natural conversational speech. For this reason, individual words were extracted from the original sentences and subsequently used as independent training examples. However, when forming the final dataset, not all presented words were used; prepositions and individual letters were excluded from the sample. This is due to their short temporal duration and low articulatory informativeness. Thus, a dataset including 22 words was formed.

The next stage was data preprocessing. For this, the original video recordings were segmented using text transcriptions and the start and end time boundaries of each command's pronunciation. Then the obtained fragments were split into frame sequences. For each frame, the region of interest (ROI) was extracted using the MediaPipe tool.

Figure 1 — Data preprocessing

At the initial stage of dataset preparation, only the lip zone was considered as the region of interest. However, during the analysis, it turned out that part of the important information about articulation is lost with this method. Although lips are the main visual component of articulation, speech is formed as a result of the interaction of several anatomical parts of the face. For example, the position of the lower jaw indicates the degree of mouth opening, and cheek movement indicates its rounding or stretching. Expanding the region of interest also increases resistance to face shifting in the frame and the speaker's facial expression features. As a result, coordinates corresponding to the chin, part of the cheeks, and the base of the nose were selected for the region of interest. Then, each frame was cropped according to the obtained region and resized to a fixed size of 96 by 96.

Figure 2 — Examples of images from the dataset

To recognize English words, a model was developed consisting of a 3D convolutional network for extracting spatio-temporal articulation features, a bidirectional LSTM for modeling temporal dependencies, a fully connected layer, and CTC decoding at the output. The CTC loss function maps input frame sequences to target labels without exact frame-by-frame correspondence between them, which is typical for the lip-reading task.

Figure 3 — Architecture of the model for English word recognition

Model quality evaluation was performed on a test sample consisting of 26 thousand examples using two metrics. The CER metric result was 14.18%, meaning 14% of characters were recognized incorrectly. The WER metric value was 23.77%, meaning 24% of words were recognized incorrectly. Thus, 76% of words were recognized completely correctly.

At the next stage, the resulting model was fine-tuned on a custom English-language dataset of mixed, noisier video recordings obtained from the Internet. This step was necessary to increase the network's generalization ability and adapt it to the specifics of the system's target application, given that Russian-language datasets recorded under ideal conditions do not exist.

Preparation of the Russian-language dataset was performed using the previously developed sequence of preprocessing methods, including video segmentation by time boundaries, ROI extraction, and conversion to fixed-size frames. The generated Russian-language dataset consists of video data collected from open Internet sources and obtained with the participation of acquaintances.

To adapt the English-language model to the Russian language, transfer learning was used. The selected model architecture consists of two main components. The first part, represented by a 3D convolutional network, extracts initial spatio-temporal articulation features, allowing it to be transferred unchanged to work with a new language. This is due to the fact that visual characteristics and dynamics of articulatory movements do not depend on a specific language, but are universal. Thus, pre-trained weights of the convolutional network can be used to adapt the entire model. The second part of the architecture, corresponding to the recurrent network and the output fully connected layer, requires mandatory fine-tuning on new data with a modified vocabulary consisting of Russian alphabet characters. Meanwhile, all other components of the model architecture remain unchanged.

Unlike intermediate fine-tuning on a mixed English-language dataset, where the dictionary of recognizable symbols and commands remained unchanged, transition to another language implies a complete change in the model's output. For this, using transfer learning, weights of the English model adapted to noisy data were transferred into the structure of the new Russian-language network. Then, weight coefficients of the output fully connected layer were deleted from parameter values because its dimension did not match the power of the Russian character dictionary. A new fully connected layer associated with the target alphabet and also taking into account a special blank symbol necessary for the correct operation of the CTC loss function was added to the architecture. Next, fine-tuning was applied. Thus, all layers of the 3D convolutional network were frozen, while parameters of the recurrent network and the updated fully connected layer changed during training.

Figure 4 — Application of transfer learning

The Adam optimizer was used to optimize parameters, distinguished by adaptive step tuning for each network weight. To preserve previously extracted features and carefully readjust the neural network to the Russian language, a reduced learning rate was chosen compared to the pre-training stage. Also, instead of the OneCycleLR scheduler, the ReduceLROnPlateau algorithm was used. Due to the fact that network fine-tuning requires only adjusting model weights rather than training from scratch, the learning rate must be changed smoothly to prevent catastrophic forgetting of previously formed features. The ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler decreases the gradient descent step only when the target recognition quality metric on the validation set stops improving. This allows all architecture components to synchronize gradually, preventing changes in frozen layer parameters and ensuring fast training of output layers.

At the current stage, training of the updated model is being carried out on the formed Russian-language dataset. The model is trained on two parts of the dataset simultaneously (Internet video recordings and custom ones), but included with varying frequencies. Moreover, more weight is given to custom recordings since they correspond most closely to the intended application conditions of the system being developed.

According to interim evaluation results at epoch 22 of training, the CER value was 13.52%, and WER was 24.17%. Model training continues. To determine the effectiveness of the chosen approach, it is required to complete training, evaluate recognition quality on a test set, analyze errors made, and make adjustments if necessary.

Also, the main functional components of the developed system for visual recognition of speech commands were determined: a video data capture and preprocessing module, a speech recognition module, and a result representation module in text and audio formats. Further work involves completing model training and evaluation, as well as implementing and testing the developed system.

Conclusions

Visual speech recognition is one of the promising areas for the development of intelligent video data processing systems and augmentative and alternative communication tools. At the same time, creating models for the Russian language is complicated by the limited availability of video data and the ambiguity of the visual representation of individual speech units. In this regard, studying the possibility of adapting pre-trained neural network models to recognize Russian speech is of practical interest. The master's thesis is devoted to the development and study of a visual recognition system for a limited set of Russian speech commands using deep learning and transfer learning methods. Within the framework of the study, the following was accomplished:

  1. A neural network model for visual recognition of English words was developed and trained based on the open GRID dataset. An experimental evaluation of the model was carried out, resulting in CER and WER metrics of 14.18% and 23.77%, respectively.
  2. A video data preprocessing pipeline was developed, including video segmentation, ROI extraction using MediaPipe, and resizing video frames to a uniform format.
  3. Additional training of the English model on video materials from open Internet sources was performed to adapt to more diverse shooting conditions.
  4. A Russian-language video dataset was formed, including materials from open Internet sources and custom video recordings.
  5. Transfer learning of the pre-trained model to the task of Russian speech recognition with adaptation of the output layer to the target alphabet was implemented. Interim model training on Russian-language data was carried out; at the twentieth epoch, CER and WER values were 13.51% and 24.8%, respectively.
  6. The main functional components of the developed system were determined, including capture and preprocessing of video data, recognition of speech commands, and output of results in text and audio formats.

Further research is directed towards the following aspects:

  1. Completion of training the Russian-language model and conducting a final quality evaluation on an independent test set.
  2. Analysis of recognition errors and investigation of the impact of training data composition on model performance.
  3. Experimental study of the effectiveness of transfer learning when adapting the model to the Russian language, including comparison with alternative training options.
  4. Implementation and verification of a software prototype of the Russian speech visual recognition system.
